# Rishta apps lose families by breaking trust

The leading matrimonial apps for Pakistani and South Asian Muslims are built on one of three chassis, and none of them was designed around the family. The first is the Indian incumbents' Matches/Search/Inbox model with an "interest, accept, pay to talk" gate (Shaadi.com, Jeevansathi, BharatMatrimony). The second is a swipe deck with photo blur and a chaperone added later (Muzz, Salams). The third is web-era pay-to-message browsing (Muslima). Dil Ka Rishta, the best-known Pakistan-only app, combines a self-serve app with a human "VIP" agent service priced at **PKR 15,000–80,000**. Users rarely complain about screens. What they report, across every app and almost every review source, is broken trust: **fake, inactive or bureau-run profiles sitting behind paywalls**, auto-renewals and refused refunds, sales calls pushing upgrades or paid verification, bans with no reason given, and security failures that make privacy controls cosmetic. In May 2026 an API flaw in Dil Ka Rishta reportedly exposed **5,663 profiles, including caste, income and photos**, despite the app advertising view-once photos and screenshot blocking. Several patterns are worth copying: Hinge's "like a specific part of the profile and add a comment" and its cap on unanswered chats, Muzz's reciprocal blur that re-blurs automatically, Shaadi's ability to verify a selfie while the photo stays hidden, verification badges that name their method, and the matchmaker-CRM "introduction" record. For a new Pakistani app, this report recommends six things. Photos should be per-person grants enforced on the server and managed inside chat. Guardians should be visible participants. Verification should be free and done before any payment. The PKR plans should be fixed-price passes that do not auto-renew, paid through JazzCash, Easypaisa or bank receipt. Bureau-managed profiles should be labelled. Bureau staff should work from a queue-driven panel with an audit log. One caveat applies throughout: almost all of the evidence comes from search-result snippets, not from the live apps, store pages or review sites.

## Search snippets, not screens, underpin this evidence

Every research thread hit the same wall. Direct page fetches failed for apps.apple.com, play.google.com, trustpilot.com, shaadi.com, muzz.com, dilkarishta.com and hinge.co, so the findings below come from search-engine snippets and summaries of those pages, not from reading them in full or using the apps. Reddit threads (r/MuslimMarriage, r/Pakistan, r/ABCDesis) did not appear in any search, so there are **no first-hand Reddit accounts** in this report. The one Reddit reference is second-hand, through a comparison blog. Many of the sources are interested parties. IslamicKnot, Zawji and Zawjni are competing marriage platforms. muzzdating.com appears to be an unofficial or affiliate site. DatingScout, Lovezoid and HealthyFramework are affiliate review sites. Many Geo.tv pieces about Dil Ka Rishta read as advertorials. App-store and Trustpilot reviews are self-selected, lean negative, and often carry inconsistent dates.

This shapes what the report can and cannot claim. It can describe each app's flows as their help pages, store listings and press coverage describe them. It cannot confirm current bottom-tab labels, the field order on match cards, Dil Ka Rishta's Urdu onboarding screens, or exact daily like limits. **No evidence was found either for or against complaints about poor Urdu or confusing UI**, because full reviews could not be read. The ranking of flaws below therefore rests on how consistently a complaint recurs across independent sources and how much harm it causes. It does not rest on review counts, which could not be measured. A hands-on teardown of each app on a mid-range Android phone is the obvious next step.

### The Indian incumbents monetise the moment two people want to talk

Shaadi.com's help pages describe the core sections as **Search, Matches, Inbox and More**. Matches is split into six overlapping lists (Preferred, Discover, Broader, 2-way, Reverse, and Maybe's & Shortlists), and the Inbox is split into Accepted, Sent, Deleted, Filtered Out and Notifications ([Shaadi.com FAQ](https://www.shaadi.com/info/customer-relations/faq/match-making)). One Play Store reviewer put the problem plainly: "my matches" are not people who matched you, just "options within your filter" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.shaadi.android&hl=en&gl=US)). All three incumbents follow the same transactional sequence. Free users browse and send or accept interests. Starting a conversation and viewing phone numbers needs a paid plan. Jeevansathi's own FAQ says only paid members can start chats or view contact details, and plans come with a quota of "Direct Contact Views" ([Jeevansathi FAQ](https://static.jeevansathi.com/P/faq_main.php?checksum=&tracepath=31)). Shaadi lets free users reply to messages that premium members start ([DatingScout](https://www.datingscout.com/shaadi/review)). This is the same asymmetry the proposed Pakistani plan uses.

Photo privacy is owner-controlled in all three apps. Shaadi offers "Only visible to members I like", Jeevansathi has a "Photos visible to" setting, and BharatMatrimony protects photos with a password the member shares on request ([BharatMatrimony FAQ](https://www.bharatmatrimony.com/faq.php)). Shaadi has one notably good detail: **the verification selfie is never displayed**, and a member can complete selfie verification while their profile photo stays hidden ([Shaadi support](https://support.shaadi.com/support/solutions/articles/155000007909-why-do-i-need-to-upload-a-profile-photo-to-complete-verification-)). Family involvement mostly means "profile created by" a parent, sibling, relative or friend ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/id480093204)), plus relationship-manager services sold by phone. **No source documented a dedicated parent dashboard or multi-login family account** in any of the three apps. Parents appear to take part by holding the login.

### Muzz and Salams put marriage features on a dating-app chassis

Muzz and Salams both use a Tinder-style swipe deck over detailed religious profiles (sect, prayer, education, height), with a selfie check at sign-up ([App Store: Salams](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/salams-halal-muslim-marriage/id965359176)). Muzz's distinctive choices are deliberate friction and privacy controls. Its help centre caps users at **five active conversations at a time**, and browsing pauses until the user unmatches someone ([Muzz help](https://muzz.com/us/en/help/muzz-101/what-happens-if-i-have-more-than-five-active-conversations/)). Another Muzz help page gives 8 chats for non-Gold and 10 for Gold, so the current rule is uncertain ([Muzz help](https://muzz.com/us/en/help/muzz-101/how-does-chat-limits-work/)). Industry press reported that swipes and revenue fell after the cap was introduced ([Online Personals Watch](https://onlinepersonalswatch.com/2024/12/muzz-introduces-chat-limit-to-encourage-meaningful-connections/)). Photo blur is free and controlled by the member. It is also **reciprocal**: a woman who blurs her own photos sees men's photos blurred too. Photos re-blur automatically after an unmatch or ban ([Muzz help](https://muzz.com/us/en/help/profile/why-are-some-profile-photos-and-videos-hidden-on-muzz/)). Muzz offers an opt-in chaperone ("Include a chaperone (or Wali) in your conversations") ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.muzmatch.muzmatchapp&hl=en_US)), but sources cannot agree on how it works. One says the wali receives weekly email transcripts, another says the chaperone joins the chat live, and a student analysis found the feature so poorly explained that many users ignore it ([IslamicKnot](https://islamicknot.com/blog/muzz-app-review-2026); [Studocu](https://www.studocu.com/my/document/universiti-teknologi-mara/business-studies/nmc105-critical-thinking-analysis-of-muzz-dating-app-features/132459351)). Muzz's safety setup is the most developed of the group: mandatory selfie, a free ID check with a blue tick, face matching to block banned users from re-registering, and chats left unencrypted so moderators can review flagged conversations ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2023/muzz-dating-app-muzmatch-growth-slows/)). Muzz matters a great deal in Pakistan. Arab News reported **over 1.5 million Pakistani users in 2025**, making Pakistan its second-largest market ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/pakistan/pakistani-singles-search-for-spouses-in-person-on-marriage-app-for-muslims-2568634)).

Salams is less clear. Its photo-privacy and wali features are disputed across sources ([Zawjni](https://zawjni.com/en/compare/muzz-vs-salams); [Swoonwise](https://swoonwise.com/reviews/salams/)), and one Trustpilot reviewer asked for wali chat access, which suggests the feature is either missing or hard to find ([Trustpilot](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/salams.app?page=3)). Muslima is the anti-model for a privacy-first app. Its privacy statement says profile information is **publicly viewable regardless of the viewer's location** ([Muslima](https://www.muslima.com/en/general/privacystatement)), messaging requires a paid plan, and reviewers describe the design as outdated ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/muslima.com?page=2)).

### Dil Ka Rishta is the local hybrid, and the local warning

Dil Ka Rishta (DKR) claims about **4 million users** ([Geo.tv](https://www.geo.tv/amp/557887-dil-ka-rishta-app-reaches-4-million-users)). It describes a four-step flow (install, create profile, get verified, connect) and an English/Urdu interface ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dil.ka.rishta&hl=en)). Verification works by uploading a CNIC (national ID card) image and a selfie under "Get Verified" ([DKR FAQs](https://www.dilkarishta.com/faqs)). The company markets women-focused photo controls: view-once images, a no-screenshots policy, and three privacy modes ("Always Public, Match-Only Reveal, Always Blurred") ([Geo.tv](https://www.geo.tv/latest/667384-introducing-dil-ka-rishta-apps-new-features); [dilkarishta.com](https://www.dilkarishta.com/llms-full.txt)). Its CEO told Dawn that the VIP service exists because many women were uncomfortable posting photos and details on a public app ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1957940)). That is direct evidence that mediated, photo-private discovery is what many Pakistani families want. VIP tiers run from **PKR 15,000 for 5 profiles to PKR 80,000 for 50** ([vip.dilkarishta.com](https://vip.dilkarishta.com/)). The self-serve Gold plan was listed at PKR 5,000 a month and discounted to PKR 2,499 ([DKR blog](https://www.dilkarishta.com/blog/silver-and-gold-plans-premium-convenience-for-dil-ka-rishta-users)). Store ratings are middling: about **3.1 on Google Play from roughly 33,700 reviews** and about 3.9 on the Pakistan App Store ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dil.ka.rishta&hl=en_US)). DKR's reply to one review said paying customers can choose to "no longer see profiles from marriage bureaus", which means bureau-run profiles are allowed in the candidate pool ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/pk/app/dil-ka-rishta/id1501708912?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)). Smaller Pakistani players fill in the rest of the market. QuickRishta advertises a photo lock with per-family access grants and guardian-managed profiles ([QuickRishta](https://quickrishta.com/online-rishta)). Rishta Dil Ka (formerly Shadi.pk) takes JazzCash and Easypaisa through the JazzCash gateway ([rishtadilka.com](https://rishtadilka.com/terms-of-use/)). Shaadi.org.pk offers sect and caste filters such as Sunni, Shia, Syed, Rajput and Pathan ([Shaadi.org.pk](https://www.shaadi.org.pk/female-proposals-islamabad-rawalpindi)).

### Hinge and Bumble set the interaction bar

Hinge requires every like to target **a specific photo or prompt**, with an optional comment. Its newsroom says this "leads to better matches and more dates than liking a profile in general" ([Hinge](https://hinge.co/newsroom/about-prompt-feedback)). It limits overload with a daily "Most Compatible" pick and, since September 2024, **Your Turn Limits**: once a user has 8 matches waiting on their reply, new profiles stop appearing until they reply or unmatch. A banner warns as the user approaches the limit, and Hinge reports about **20% higher responsiveness** in testing (a company figure) ([Yahoo Lifestyle](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/hinge-adds-limit-unanswered-messages-120000786.html)). Bumble's 2024 "Opening Moves" lets women set a question that men answer first ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/bumbles-opening-move-feature-takes-the-pressure-off-women-to-come-up-with-a-new-message-every-time/)). Bumble added a filter for ID-verified profiles in 2025 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-03-17/bumble-rolls-out-id-verification-in-dating-app-safety-push)), and its Private Detector blurs explicit images received in chat, with view, delete and report options in place ([Bumble Support](https://support.bumble.com/hc/articles/32668018305181-Private-Detector)).

There is a fair counter-argument behind several of these flaws. Pay-to-talk gating pays for moderation and verification, and Muzz's chat cap shows that a market leader will knowingly give up swipes and revenue to signal seriousness. The trade-offs are real. Muzz chose not to fully encrypt chats so that moderators can review flagged conversations ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2023/muzz-dating-app-muzmatch-growth-slows/)), and a family-oriented app faces the same choice. The lesson is not "never monetise contact". It is narrower: **charging for access only works when the pool is trustworthy**, and the incumbents lost that legitimacy by charging before they had earned it.

## Hinge, Muzz and matchmaker CRMs already solved pieces

The best patterns are scattered across apps that were not built for Pakistani families. Each one needs adapting rather than copying.

The most valuable interaction pattern is Hinge's **targeted interest with a comment**. A rishta app can make every "Send interest" point at a specific prompt answer ("What a good weekend with family looks like", "Values I want in our home") and carry an optional note. That gives the recipient, and their family, a reason to reply. Hinge's text-forward layout, which interleaves prompts with photos, also matters more here than in dating. When photos are hidden by default, profiles have to work as readable documents, and Hinge's redesign deliberately used typography to draw attention to written answers ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/hinge-redesigns-to-get-people-off-dating-apps/)). Two anti-ghosting mechanics have documented results. Muzz's five-chat cap ([Muzz help](https://muzz.com/us/en/help/muzz-101/how-many-chats-conversations-can-i-have/)) and Hinge's 8-pending-reply limit with an "approaching the limit" banner ([Yahoo Lifestyle](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/hinge-adds-limit-unanswered-messages-120000786.html)) both make responsiveness a condition of continuing to browse. That matters because paying members' main complaint about free members is that they never reply.

On privacy, the reference points are Muzz's **reciprocal blur and automatic re-blur on unmatch or block** ([Muzz help](https://muzz.com/us/en/help/profile/why-are-some-profile-photos-and-videos-hidden-on-muzz/)), QuickRishta's per-family photo grants ([QuickRishta](https://quickrishta.com/online-rishta)), and Shaadi's separation of the verification selfie from the visible profile photo ([Shaadi support](https://support.shaadi.com/support/solutions/articles/155000007908-is-my-data-safe-will-my-selfie-be-shared-)). On consent, Hinge's Match Note (a match only completes once the other person has viewed the note) ([Hinge](https://hinge.co/press/match-note)) and Bumble's 24-hour decision window after an Opening Move ([ABC7](https://abc7chicago.com/do-girls-have-to-message-first-on-bumble-dating-app-opening-moves/14750847/)) show how to give the receiving side control without adding much friction. On safety inside chat, Bumble's blurred incoming images with view, delete and report options ([Bumble Support](https://support.bumble.com/hc/articles/32668018305181-Private-Detector)) and Tinder's "Are You Sure?" prompt before an offensive message is sent, which cut inappropriate language by more than 10% in early testing (a company claim) ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2021/05/20/tinder-offensive-messages?stream=top)), are cheap and proven. On trust, the industry vocabulary has settled on two layers of badges: selfie liveness for everyone, plus a separate ID badge. Hinge models honest wording: its Selfie Verified badge explicitly "does not guarantee the safety of a user", and Hinge says it deletes facial templates, usually within 24 hours ([Hinge Help](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/10303221435539-What-is-Selfie-Verification-)). Hinge also tells users how dealbreakers and "Prefer Not to Say" affect who can see them ([Hinge](https://hinge.co/newsroom/hinge-2025-product-evolution)). A rishta app needs the same openness about how caste and sect filters affect visibility.

The back-office patterns come from two separate tool families, and a bureau needs both. Indian matrimony scripts handle moderation and money well: pending-profile and photo approval queues, manual payment-proof review, per-staff permissions, franchise admins, CSV/Excel member import and sales reports ([Narjis Enterprise](https://narjisenterprise.com/advance-matrimonial-script-1.3); [MobileOnline Matrimony Pro](https://fair.pm/packages/plugins/mobileonline-matrimony-pro/)). Western matchmaker CRMs handle relationships well. SmartMatchApp treats an **introduction as a first-class record**: the client reviews it in a portal and gives feedback, and a "mutual" dashboard shows every introduction between two parties ([SmartMatchApp](https://smartmatchapp.com/en/blog/posts/how-to-master-introductions-presets-match-automation-in-your-matchmaking-software)). Content-moderation tools add throughput habits: arrow-key navigation through queues, a keyboard shortcut to resolve an item, and bulk actions limited to clear, low-risk cases ([Stream](https://getstream.io/resources/projects/moderation-course/moderator/bulk-actions/); [Moderation API](https://moderationapi.com/blog/content-queue-improvements)). Some Pakistani bureaus already use trust signals worth copying, such as Asli Rishta's success fee "fixed in writing before any introduction" ([aslirishta.pk](https://aslirishta.pk/)).

## A blueprint for a privacy-first Pakistani rishta app

The recommendations below turn the evidence into specifications for the planned product. Where a choice is design judgement and not a sourced finding, the text says so.

### Navigation built around a family's shortlist, not a swipe deck

Five bottom tabs fit the use case: **Rishtay** (one recommended feed plus search), **Interests** (received, sent, accepted), **Chats**, **Family** (linked guardians, a shared shortlist, guardian activity) and **Profile**. This deliberately rejects both Shaadi's six overlapping match lists and the swipe deck. Parents browse, compare and discuss, and a shortlist suits that better than a left/right gesture. Profile cards should lead with what families screen on first: first name or initials, age, city, education and profession, sect, and biradari if the member chose to show it. Then come the verification badges, "Profile managed by: Mother" where it applies, a "last active" indicator and one standout prompt. The photo slot shows a neutral illustrated placeholder, never a blurred real photo, because a blur still leaks skin tone and silhouette (a design judgement). Filters should separate hard dealbreakers from soft preferences, show live result counts, and follow Hinge in warning when a filter or a "prefer not to say" answer will hide the user from others. Caste, biradari and sect should be filterable but never mandatory, which matches local practice of using caste filters when a family has a clear preference without piling on conditions ([Weddme.pk](https://weddme.pk/blog/sahiwal-rishta-guide-for-ideal-match)).

Onboarding should be short themed steps with a segmented progress bar: phone OTP; "Who is creating this profile?" (self, parent, sibling, relative); basics; family background; religion and values; prompts; photos (uploaded privately, with no visibility choice to get wrong); and verification. The last two steps should be skippable and resumable through a profile-strength checklist. NN/g's guidance applies to the first-run experience: blank empty states leave users unsure whether the system is working ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design)). The "no new rishtay today" screen should explain why and offer one next step. It should not offer an upsell.

### Photos stay private until a named person is granted access in chat

The planned rule (no photos by default; request, approve and revoke inside chat) is stricter than any app studied, and it must be enforced on the server to mean anything. DKR is the cautionary case: client-side view-once and screenshot blocking did not stop an API that returned photos to anyone who changed an ID number. Every photo should be stored privately. The profile API should never include a photo URL, and the photo endpoint should check for an active grant between this viewer and this owner on every request, then return a signed URL valid for minutes, with a per-viewer watermark. Screenshot blocking (Android FLAG_SECURE) is worth turning on, but the app should tell users plainly that it cannot stop a second phone's camera.

| State | Who can act | What the requester sees | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| No access (default) | Requester can tap "Request photos" once the chat is open | Illustrated placeholder | Request appears as a system card in the chat, never as a push notification that shows the requester's photo |
| Requested | Owner (and, if the owner chooses, a linked guardian) can Approve, Decline or "Not yet" | "Request sent" status | One open request at a time; a decline puts the button on a cooldown, to prevent pestering |
| Granted | Owner can revoke at any time from the chat header | Photos, watermarked with the viewer's ID, in the chat and on the profile | Optional time-boxed grants (e.g. 7 days); both sides see a "Photos shared" banner |
| Revoked / expired | Automatic on block, report, unmatch or chat closure | Placeholder again; cached images purged | Mirrors Muzz's automatic re-blur on unmatch |

Two further rules follow from the evidence. Photo privacy should be **reciprocal by default**, as on Muzz, so members who keep their photos hidden cannot view others' photos without a mutual exchange. And no plan should ever let a member filter out or rank down photo-private profiles. That is the Muzz Gold mistake, and it quietly penalises the women the privacy feature exists to protect.

### Guardians join as visible participants, not hidden recipients

Guardian involvement should be a linked account, not a shared password and not a weekly transcript nobody understands. A candidate invites a guardian by phone number. The guardian gets a defined role: view the profile and shared shortlist, suggest or send interests on the candidate's behalf, and, only if the candidate turns it on, read chats or co-approve photo grants. Any chat a guardian can see shows a persistent **"Wali present"** indicator to both sides. This answers the confusion over Muzz's chaperone and respects the other party's right to know who is reading. A parent-managed profile should say so on the card ("Managed by: Father"), an expectation the market has already set ([App Store: Shaadi](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/id480093204); [QuickRishta](https://quickrishta.com/online-rishta)). An in-app **"Speak to my wali"** handoff should start a voice or video call with the linked guardian through the app. That removes the main reason people swap numbers, which is the behaviour that got a Muzz user banned. Guardian seats should be free on every plan. Charging for family participation would turn the product's main differentiator into an upsell.

### Verification is free, upfront and explains its own method

Verification happens before any payment, never after, which fixes Jeevansathi's documents-after-payment problem and DKR's reported verification upsell calls. Use four distinct badges, each of which opens a sheet saying what was checked and when, plus Hinge-style "not a guarantee of safety" wording: **Phone verified**; **CNIC + selfie verified** (liveness match, CNIC checked by staff); **Bureau verified** (an in-person or video interview with bureau staff, done by a named officer on a recorded date); and **Guardian linked**. The verification selfie, as on Shaadi, is never shown on the profile. Store only the outcome (method, verifier, date, last four CNIC digits) and delete CNIC images after review. Pakistan still has **no enacted comprehensive data protection law** ([Friday Times](https://www.thefridaytimes.com/11-Dec-2025/data-privacy-peril-pakistan-s-legal-vacuum-consequences)), and the PTA has acknowledged that leaked CNIC datasets circulating in the country involved "numerous mobile applications" ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2565846/pta-denies-involvement-in-reported-data-leaks)), so a retained CNIC image is a liability, not an asset. Offer a "verified only" discovery filter, following Bumble, at no charge.

### PKR passes that end instead of renewing

The planned prices sit sensibly in the local market. **Basic at PKR 4,000 for 3 months is about PKR 1,333 a month**, below DKR's Silver promotional price of PKR 1,499 a month. **Premium at PKR 8,500 is about PKR 2,833 a month**, slightly above DKR Gold's PKR 2,499 promotional price and well below its PKR 5,000 list price ([DKR blog](https://www.dilkarishta.com/blog/silver-and-gold-plans-premium-convenience-for-dil-ka-rishta-users)). Both are a small fraction of the PKR 15,000–80,000 that agent services charge. The quotas below are suggested starting points to test, not sourced benchmarks.

| Capability | Free | Basic (PKR 4,000 / 3 mo) | Premium (PKR 8,500 / 3 mo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profile, prompts, private photos, guardian seats, all verification badges, block/report | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Browse, search, core filters, "verified only" filter, shortlist | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Accept or decline interests from paying members, and chat in those threads (including photo request/approve/revoke) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Send interests (targeted at a prompt, optional note) | No; free members' shortlists notify the paying member ("Someone shortlisted you") | e.g. 5 per day | e.g. 15 per day |
| Advanced filters (biradari, relocation, family details) and "who viewed or shortlisted me" | No | Yes | Yes |
| Priority placement, labelled honestly as "Featured" | No | No | Yes |
| Bureau-assisted introductions by staff | No | No | e.g. 2 curated introductions per month, logged in the app |

The free-reply rule copies Shaadi's asymmetry, and Shaadi's paying users complain that free users do not reply. Four mitigations follow from the evidence. First, interests expire after 14 days, and the sender's interest credit is returned when that happens. Second, a Hinge-style "your turn" limit pauses a free member's browsing when several paid interests are waiting for an answer. Third, cards show a reply-rate indicator ("Usually replies within 2 days"). Fourth, a published rule extends or refunds a pass when a paying member gets no accepted interests within a stated period. That rule directly answers Shaadi's "you received responses" refund refusals, and the Match Group settlement shows why any guarantee's conditions must be stated in plain words before purchase ([FKKS](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102l0pi/ftc-swipes-left-on-match-groups-deceptive-advertising-and-cancellation-practices)).

On payments, prices should be fixed, with no permanent "50% off" framing. The checkout screen should show the total, the duration and the end date, and plans should **never auto-renew**, with reminders 7 days and 1 day before expiry. Wallets make this natural. JazzCash had about **21 million monthly active users** and Easypaisa about **18 million** as of August 2025 ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1931181)), so both belong at launch, alongside bank transfer. The manual flow should show the exact amount and a unique order reference to include in the transfer note. The member uploads the receipt screenshot and transaction ID against that order, and the order moves from Pending to Verified to Active, with a stated review time. A gateway integration can replace manual review later. Safety features, photo controls, guardian seats and verification should never sit behind the paywall.

### Chat, notifications and Urdu tuned for Pakistani phones

Chat should include free in-app voice and video with no number exchange, which Muzz has made the standard ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/?p=781575)). Add an Opening-Move-style option where women (or their guardians) set a first question, "Are You Sure?" prompts before an offensive message is sent, blurred incoming images, and report/block reachable from the chat header in two taps. Phone numbers are released only when both sides tap "Share contact". A bureau-wide rule should hold that **staff never release contact details on one-sided approval**, which follows VIP Shaadi's promise that details are never shared without consent ([Shaadi support](https://support.shaadi.com/support/solutions/articles/48000962510-what-is-vip-shaadi-service-)).

Notifications should follow NN/g's guidance: ask for permission at a moment of value (after the first interest is sent) rather than on first launch, and say what notifications will contain ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/push-notification/)). Instant pushes should cover interests received or accepted, messages, photo requests and grants, guardian events, payment verification and plan expiry. Profile views and shortlists go in a daily digest. Notifications should clear once read and land on the right screen, which fixes the BharatMatrimony and Shaadi complaints. As a design judgement, lock-screen previews should default to content-free text ("You have a new rishta message"), because phones are often shared within households.

Language support has to follow how Pakistanis actually type. Roman Urdu is the de facto digital writing standard, and users avoid Urdu script when typing ([arXiv 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.06008)). Offer an English and Urdu interface, make search and caste or city matching tolerant of varied Roman Urdu spellings, and let chat accept any script. Urdu screens need their own type scale. Nastaliq body text needs a line-height of about **2.2 or more**, and cards must not clip overflow ([rtlfonts.com](https://rtlfonts.com/blog/nastaliq-web-typography-css)), so a Naskh-style font suits dense labels and inputs. RTL mirroring should flip layout and directional icons, while phone numbers, prices, ages and heights stay left-to-right ([Apple WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10107/)). Performance on mid-range and low-end Android needs real effort despite cheap data, given DKR's crash and slow-loading reviews: detect low-RAM devices and serve smaller images, and aim for start-up under 5 seconds ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/androidgo/best-practices)).

### A bureau console built around queues, introductions and audit

Pakistan has an estimated **33,000 marriage bureaus in 210 cities** (DKR's own sizing) ([Profit](https://issuu.com/pakistantoday-paperazzi/docs/profit_221024/s/17170784)). The bureaus run on WhatsApp groups and Facebook pages, and the press has documented their failures: a matchmaker telling a reporter "it's not my job to verify" ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/magazine/you/1433784-paid-introductions-unchecked-truths)), and scammers impersonating bureaus to collect Easypaisa payments ([RishtayWalay Center](https://rishtaywalycenter.online/)). The admin panel is where the new app can be visibly better than both apps and bureaus.

| Module | Core design |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | Work queues at the top, each with age and service-level indicators: profiles pending approval, photos pending, CNIC checks pending, payment proofs pending, introductions awaiting the other side's response, overdue follow-ups. Below them: the funnel (registered → verified → paid), revenue collected vs pending by method, and passes expiring in the next 7/30 days |
| **Approvals** | Side-by-side review: the submitted profile (with a diff for edits) next to photos and automated flags (duplicate phone or CNIC, face mismatch, banned words). Keyboard actions for approve, reject with a fixed reason code, request changes, skip. Bulk actions only for low-risk tasks such as tagging or assigning |
| **Verification** | CNIC images viewed in a restricted pane and deleted after a decision; the record keeps method, officer and date; the "Bureau verified" badge is issued only by named officers |
| **Payments** | One order per purchase, with every uploaded proof attached to that order (avoiding the duplicate-entry problem a CodeCanyon buyer reported) ([CodeCanyon](https://codecanyon.net/comments/31712803)). The expected amount, plan and order reference sit beside the receipt; duplicate transaction IDs are detected automatically; one approve/reject decision sets the pass's start and end dates |
| **Manual matchmaking** | An Introduction record (client A, client B, proposing staff member, status, each side's response, feedback, next follow-up date). The client's hard preferences are pinned next to each candidate, with a per-criterion fit check; profiles already sent or declined are excluded; a per-client quota tracker (e.g. "2 of 2 this month sent"); two-sided consent before contact details are released |
| **Offline clients (WhatsApp)** | Create records from a form, CSV/Excel import or pasted WhatsApp biodata text; tag source = WhatsApp; deduplicate on phone/CNIC; record consent (a confirmation link sent to the client); visibility defaults to **bureau-only**; if published, the card carries a "Bureau-managed" label; photos go into the same private grant system |
| **Access and audit** | Roles (super-admin, verification officer, finance, matchmaker, branch admin) see only what they need. Contact details and documents are masked by default; revealing them requires a reason and is logged. An append-only audit log covers views, exports, approvals, edits and contact releases. CSV export is limited to super-admins and watermarked |

The matchmaking module directly targets the documented failures of assisted services, where profiles were sent "just for count", already-seen profiles were resent, and clients heard nothing back from the other side ([ConsumerComplaints](https://www.consumercomplaints.in/bharat-matrimony-disappointing-assisted-service-c3382635)). The audit design borrows from India's DPDP Rules 2025, which require access controls and logging, and which secondary legal summaries say require processing logs to be kept for at least a year ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/dpdp-rules-2025-india-data-protection-law-compliance-40769.html/)). Pakistan has no equivalent statute yet, so adopting that standard voluntarily is both protection and a selling point. The offline-client import design is a recommendation built from first principles. No source documented how any bureau tool handles WhatsApp-origin clients.

## Conclusion

The central finding is that in this market **restraint is the product**. The interface features that create trust are mostly refusals: no auto-renewal, no paid phone-number quotas, no paying to filter out privacy-conscious women, no paid verification, no outbound sales calls, and no bureau profiles passed off as candidates. Each refusal costs short-term revenue, and Muzz's chat cap shows a market leader accepting that trade. But the evidence suggests the incumbents' revenue tactics are what produced the fake-profile, billing and scam reputation that now dominates their reviews. A new Pakistani app that refuses them publicly, and enforces photo privacy on the server rather than in marketing copy, can compete on trust against DKR's leak and against global apps that underserve Pakistan-based users.

The second implication is strategic. The bureau console is not an internal tool attached to a consumer app. It may be the defensible core. Bureaus already hold the offline families, the WhatsApp clients and the in-person verification capacity that apps lack, while apps hold the structured data and audit trail that bureaus lack. Combining them with labelled profiles, two-sided consent and logged staff access turns the "rishta aunty" model critics compare DKR's VIP tier to into something accountable. Before committing to build, the team should close the evidence gaps this report could not: hands-on teardowns of DKR, Muzz and Shaadi on a mid-range Android phone; Reddit and Urdu-language user feedback; and confirmation of current tab structures, Urdu screen quality and JazzCash/Easypaisa subscription flows.
